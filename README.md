## 🧐 What Does This Tool Do?

Think of junction folders as magical portals. You move a big folder (like your Downloads or Documents) to another drive, and then create a junction that makes Windows think the folder is still where it was. Your programs and files work exactly as before, but you've freed up valuable space on your main drive.

**Example:** Your C: drive is full, but your D: drive has plenty of room. You move your "Downloads" folder to D:\Downloads, then use this tool to create a junction at C:\Users\YourName\Downloads that points to D:\Downloads. Everything works perfectly, but you've gained gigabytes of free space!

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Is this safe for my files?

Yes! Junction folders are a standard Windows feature. Your files are physically moved to the new location, and the junction simply tells Windows where to find them. Nothing gets deleted or corrupted.

### Will my programs still work?

Absolutely. Programs don't know the difference – they just see the folder path they expect. That's the beauty of junction folders.

### Can I use this with OneDrive or cloud folders?

Generally yes, but be careful. Cloud sync services sometimes get confused by junctions. Test with a small folder first.

### What happens if I delete the junction?

Your files remain safe in their new location. You'll just need to create a new junction or access them directly from the new path.

### Do I need administrator rights?

Yes, creating junction folders requires administrator privileges. If you're not an admin, you'll need to ask someone who is.

## ⚠️ Troubleshooting

**"Access Denied" error?** – Make sure you're running the tool as administrator. Right-click the application and select "Run as administrator."

**"Path not found" error?** – Double-check that your destination folder exists. You might need to create it first.

**Junction not working?** – Try restarting Windows Explorer (right-click taskbar → Task Manager → Windows Explorer → Restart) or simply restart your computer.

**Can't find my files?** – Check the destination folder directly. Your files are physically there now, even though they appear in the original location too.
